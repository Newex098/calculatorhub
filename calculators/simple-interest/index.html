What is Simple Interest?

Simple interest is a straightforward, linear method of calculating the interest charge on a borrowed loan or the return earned on an invested deposit. Unlike compound interest, simple interest is computed exclusively on the original principal amount for the entire duration of the agreement.

Because interest earned in past periods is never added back to the principal, the amount of simple interest accrued in year one is identical to the interest accrued in year ten. This creates a steady, predictable linear growth pattern.

How Simple Interest Works

Simple interest operates on three core financial parameters:

  • Principal (P): The initial amount of money borrowed or deposited.
  • Annual Interest Rate (R): The percentage rate charged or paid by the financial institution per year.
  • Time Period (T): The loan tenure or deposit duration, converted to years.

The Simple Interest Formula

The fundamental mathematical formulas for simple interest are:

SI = (P × R × T) / 100

Total Maturity Amount: A = P + SI

Where each variable represents:

  • SI: Total Simple Interest accrued over the entire period
  • A: Total maturity amount receivable or payable (P + SI)
  • P: Principal investment or loan balance in Rupees (₹)
  • R: Annual nominal interest rate in percentage (e.g. 8 for 8%)
  • T: Time duration in years (decimal values like 1.5 years are supported)

Time Period (T) Conversion by Duration Unit

Because annual interest rates (R) are quoted on a per-year basis, any duration specified in months or days must be converted to years before applying the simple interest formula:

  • When duration is in Years: T = Years
  • When duration is in Months: T = Months / 12 (e.g., 18 months = 18 / 12 = 1.5 years)
  • When duration is in Days: T = Days / 365 (e.g., 180 days = 180 / 365 ≈ 0.49315 years)

Note: Day-based calculations use a standard 365-day year convention. Actual financial products may use different terms or day-count conventions (such as 360 days or actual/actual).

Step-by-Step Worked Examples

Example 1 (Years): ₹10,000 principal at 8% annual interest for 5 years
  • 1
    Identify Inputs: Principal P = ₹10,000, Annual Rate R = 8%, Time T = 5 Years.
  • 2
    Calculate Annual Interest: (10,000 × 8) / 100 = ₹800/year.
  • 3
    Multiply by Total Years: SI = ₹800 × 5 = ₹4,000.
  • 4
    Calculate Final Maturity Value: A = P + SI = ₹10,000 + ₹4,000 = ₹14,000.
Example 2 (Months): ₹50,000 principal at 9% annual interest for 18 months
  • 1
    Convert Months to Years: T = 18 / 12 = 1.5 Years.
  • 2
    Calculate Simple Interest: SI = (50,000 × 9 × 1.5) / 100 = ₹6,750.
  • 3
    Calculate Final Maturity Value: A = P + SI = ₹50,000 + ₹6,750 = ₹56,750.
Example 3 (Days): ₹1,00,000 principal at 10% annual interest for 180 days
  • 1
    Convert Days to Years (365-day convention): T = 180 / 365 ≈ 0.49315 Years.
  • 2
    Calculate Simple Interest: SI = (1,00,000 × 10 × 180) / (100 × 365) = ₹4,931.51.
  • 3
    Calculate Final Maturity Value: A = P + SI = ₹1,00,000 + ₹4,931.51 = ₹1,04,931.51.

When is Simple Interest Commonly Used?

While long-term wealth investments typically utilize compound interest, simple interest remains widely utilized across several key sectors:

  • Automobile Loans: Most dealer and retail car financing contracts calculate monthly amortization based on fixed simple interest.
  • Short-Term Consumer Loans: Personal promissory notes, bridge financing, and micro-loans often charge simple daily or monthly interest.
  • Certificates of Deposit & Non-Cumulative FDs: Fixed deposits that distribute regular quarterly or annual interest payouts to the investor rather than reinvesting use simple interest calculations.
  • Education Loans: Certain student loan structures accrue simple interest while the student remains enrolled in university.
